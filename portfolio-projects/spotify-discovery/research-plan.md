# Spotify Discovery Sprint — user research plan

**Status:** Planned, not conducted. No findings are invented.

## Research questions
1. In what listening moments does finding something new feel like work?
2. What is the difference between active discovery and background listening?
3. How do users describe the last failed recommendation?
4. Which input is easier: mood, activity, time available or prompt text?
5. What makes a recommended track worth saving?

## Participants and sampling
12 proposed participants: 6 frequent discoverers, 4 occasional and 2 returning listeners; recruit across different age groups, accessibility needs and listening contexts. Avoid claiming representativeness. Screen for recent discovery behaviour, not brand loyalty.

## Methods
- 30-minute semi-structured interviews based on last real listening moment.
- Task walkthrough of currently available discovery paths (consent-based, no private accounts recorded).
- 5–6 moderated prototype usability tests comparing standard browsing vs three-choice Sprint.
- Diary check-in over one week **only if** early evidence warrants it.

## Interview prompts (non-leading)
- “Tell me about the last time you wanted new music.”
- “Show me how you chose what to listen to.”
- “What happened when you did not like the first suggestion?”
- “What did you do after finding a good track?”
- “How would you decide that a recommendation was useful?”

## Research synthesis
Create evidence records with: observation, anonymised quote, behaviour, severity, confidence, segment and alternative explanation. Convert to opportunities only after pattern review.

## Assumption and test table
| Hypothesis | Evidence needed | Failure signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decision fatigue blocks exploration | Repeated observed delays/abandonment | Most users enjoy open browsing |
| Intent chips reduce first-play time | Prototype task timings | No meaningful improvement |
| Three choices improve confidence | Think-aloud and save rate | Users feel constrained |
| Explanation builds trust | Preference and repeat-use behaviour | Added clutter and slower decisions |

## Ethics
Consent, voluntary participation, no recording without permission, no sensitive profile scraping, anonymised notes.

## Illustrative synthesis, NOT findings
**If** participants value serendipity more than speed, prioritise “surprise me” and drop rigid intent filtering. The decision changes with the evidence.
