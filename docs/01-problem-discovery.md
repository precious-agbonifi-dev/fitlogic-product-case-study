# Problem discovery and assumptions

## Context
People starting or restarting fitness routines face a gap between knowing they should exercise and building an achievable routine. FitLogic is a proposed B2C app to help users select a realistic plan, complete sessions and see progress.

## Problem statement
**When** beginners have changing schedules, **they need** a realistic weekly workout plan that is easy to follow, **so that** they can build consistency without extensive research or constant manual planning.

## Jobs to be done
- Help me decide what workout to do today.
- Let me fit exercise around my working week.
- Show me evidence of consistent progress.
- Make it easy to restart after missing a session.

## Assumptions to validate
| Assumption | Risk | Validation approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Beginners struggle more with consistency than exercise variety | High | 8–12 discovery interviews |
| Goal-led onboarding helps users start faster | Medium | Moderated prototype tests |
| Weekly progress feedback increases engagement | High | Small pilot with controlled comparison |
| Users would pay for guidance | High | Pricing interviews and landing-page test |

## Discovery questions
1. Tell me about the last time you tried to follow a fitness programme.
2. What made it difficult to stay consistent?
3. How did you choose your next workout?
4. What tools or services have you already tried?
5. When did you last decide not to train, and why?

Avoid asking leading questions such as “Would you use FitLogic?”

## Evidence status
These are hypotheses informed by fitness coaching experience, not validated research findings. Interview results should be documented with consent, anonymised and distinguished from initial assumptions.
